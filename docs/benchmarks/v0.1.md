# Benchmarks: v0.1 against hand-written wiring

Task T1.9, 2026-11-01. This page measures Trellis at commit de3a8d8 (T1.1 to T1.5: bindings, modules, deduced constructors, storage and `create<T>()`) against hand-written wiring of the same programs, and against the targets of [plan §3.2](../plan.md#32-non-functional-targets), with GCC 13 and Clang 18. The equivalence oracle checks that Trellis builds the same object graphs. The benchmark variants are in commit 4b882c9, and the commands at the end reproduce every number.

Three budgets are missed, and `budgets.json` is unchanged. The owner decides what follows; the analysis is in "Where the time and the bytes go".

- **NFR-04, by a wide margin on both compilers.** The ratio comes from the code generated per singleton. About two thirds of Trellis's extra compile time at 100 components goes to optimization and code generation, and a third to template instantiation. At this size the headers cost almost nothing.
- **NFR-06, on Clang.** The cost comes from the thread-safe slot. Trellis adds 3.4 KB (GCC) and 4.3 KB (Clang) to the binary, and the linker's page-aligned segments round that up to 8 and 12 KiB. With `threading::none` the binary grows by 0 and 4,096 bytes.
- **NFR-03, on Clang, by 0.01 s at the median.** Single measurements fall on both sides of the budget, on both compilers. Clang's cost is about 0.13 s of headers and 0.17 to 0.32 s of wiring.

The remaining verdicts:

- **NFR-06 on GCC.** "8 KB" is read here as 8,192 bytes, so GCC is exactly at the limit. The plan writes the hand-wired baseline's 18,736 bytes as 18.7 KB, which suggests 1,000-byte kilobytes; read that way, GCC's +8,192 B is also over, by 192 B [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: which kilobyte NFR-06 means].
- **NFR-01 is met with little margin on GCC.** The runs that no other process disturbed give 1.06 to 1.08×, and the fastest repetition over 11 runs gives 1.07×. Clang is level with the hand-written wiring.
- **NFR-02,** which T1.9 doesn't ask for, is over its 1.5× target on both compilers (1.56 to 1.60×). Building the injector and its two singletons costs about 47 ns more than two `std::make_shared` calls.
- **Not measured:** NFR-04 at 1,000 components (see "Growth"), and NFR-05's layouts (plan §8.4: one module per 10 components, several translation units), for which the harness has no option yet.

## What was measured

### The two wirings

Every program exists twice: wired by hand, which is the baseline of every target, and wired with Trellis. Both build the same classes, and the classes know nothing of either wiring. Both wirings are the shapes the research measured, and Trellis uses only its public API with its defaults: deduced constructors, lazy singletons and `threading::safe`.

- **The scenario** (`benchmarks/scenario/`). This is the research scenario: an `App` with a `Service`, a shared logger and repository, a new clock per request and an external `Config`. `manual.hpp` wires it by hand. `trellis.hpp` wires it with one binding per decision that the hand-written wiring makes, then calls `create<App>()`:

  ```cpp
  trellis::make_injector(
      trellis::bind<Config>.to_ref(config),
      trellis::bind<ILogger>.to<CountingLogger>().in(trellis::singleton),
      trellis::bind<IRepository>.to<InMemoryRepository>().in(trellis::singleton),
      trellis::bind<IClock>.to<SteadyClock>());
  ```

  `manual.cpp` and `trellis.cpp` are the same program with a different wiring class. They are the subject of NFR-03 and NFR-06. The runtime benchmark (`benchmarks/runtime/scenario_bench.cpp`) measures the same two classes for NFR-01 and NFR-02.
- **The generated graphs** (`benchmarks/compile/gen_graph.py`). This is the research harness's graph: N components, each with an interface, each a singleton requested as `std::shared_ptr`, each depending on 1 to 3 earlier ones. The `manual` variant creates the singletons with `std::make_shared`, in dependency order. The `trellis` variant is one `make_injector` call, with `trellis::bind<Ik>.to<Ck>().in(trellis::singleton)` per component (the research wired Boost.DI the same way), followed by `create<Root>()`. The graphs are the subject of NFR-04 and NFR-05.

### Method

- **Compile time, memory and size** (NFR-03 to NFR-06): `scripts/bench-compile.sh --quick`. It runs `benchmarks/compile/run.py` on the scenario and on graphs of N = 10, 50 and 100, with gcc-13 and clang-18.
  - Each program is compiled 3 times in a row, the hand-written program just before the Trellis one, and the median wall time is the result.
  - The compiler is called directly, never through ccache, one compile at a time, after one untimed compile that loads the compiler and the standard headers into the page cache.
  - The flags are those the research used: `-std=c++20 -O2 -Wall -Wextra` for the scenario and `-std=c++20 -O2` for the graphs.
  - Peak memory is the compiler's largest resident set over the 3 compiles (`wait4`).
  - The binary is linked with `-O2` and stripped with `strip --strip-all`; the text size is what `size` reports.
- **NFR-03's repeated measurements.** NFR-03 is a difference of about 0.3 s between two compiles of about 2 s, and on this machine single compiles vary by ±0.2 s. It was therefore measured 9 times: 6 runs of the script's median of 3, and 3 runs of a measurement that alternates three compiles, 7 or 15 times each:
  - `manual.cpp`;
  - `manual.cpp` with `<trellis/trellis.hpp>` included, which is the header alone;
  - `trellis.cpp`.

  The difference of the second and the first is the header's cost, and that of the third and the second is the wiring's.
- **Run time** (NFR-01, NFR-02): `scripts/bench-runtime.sh`. It uses Google Benchmark 1.8.3, built with the gcc-13-release and clang-18-release presets at `-O2 -DNDEBUG`, pinned to the last CPU, with 7 repetitions of 200,000 iterations per case. The result is the median of the 7.
  - `resolve` creates the `App` from an injector whose singletons already exist (NFR-01).
  - `graph` creates the injector, resolves the `App` and destroys everything (NFR-02).

  A run takes about 2 seconds, so it was repeated, and runs that another process disturbed are shown as such.
- **Where the time goes:**
  - The 100-component graph's two wirings, 3 compiles each.
  - Clang's `-ftime-trace` of both. Each microsecond is counted once, in the innermost trace event that holds it (self time), and grouped by the template or phase it belongs to.
  - GCC's `-ftime-report`.
- **Load.** The container's 4 vCPUs are shared with other agents' builds, which inflate compile times. Measurements started only after the 1-minute load average had been below 1.5 for two readings 30 s apart, except two analysis runs that followed straight on and are shown with their load. The load was sampled every 5 s while each ran. The figures include the measurement's own compiler, which counts for about 1. Builds that other agents started during a measurement are noted next to its numbers.

## The equivalence oracle

`tests/property/check_sharing.py` (plan §9.1) wires random graphs both ways and checks each program's output line against the line that `gen_graph.py`'s model of the graph predicts. The line holds two things:

- an identity checksum over a walk of the three roots (two from one injector, one from a second), which shows which requests share an object;
- the number of objects built, which must equal the number reached.

Both wirings must print the model's line for every graph, so Trellis's checksums equal the hand-wired ones. Since T1.9 both wirings run by default: on the 32 local seeds, and on CI's fresh seeds (64 per push, 256 nightly under TSan).

The run on 200 graphs used seeds 1 to 200 and the clang-18-release preset, which compiles with `-O3 -DNDEBUG` and the project warnings as errors:

```bash
TRELLIS_PROPERTY_SEEDS=1-200 ctest --preset clang-18-release -L property --timeout 3000
```

```text
PASS property.sharing (manual, trellis, clang-18): 100 of 100 graphs share objects as their lifetimes say (seeds 1, 5, 9, ..., 197; 50 seeds)
PASS property.sharing (manual, trellis, clang-18): 100 of 100 graphs share objects as their lifetimes say (seeds 2, 6, 10, ..., 198; 50 seeds)
PASS property.sharing (manual, trellis, clang-18): 100 of 100 graphs share objects as their lifetimes say (seeds 3, 7, 11, ..., 199; 50 seeds)
PASS property.sharing (manual, trellis, clang-18): 100 of 100 graphs share objects as their lifetimes say (seeds 4, 8, 12, ..., 200; 50 seeds)
100% tests passed, 0 tests failed out of 5
```

The oracle's self-test passed in the same run: each of its four planted sharing bugs is caught in 4 of 4 graphs. The run started at load 5.9 and took 608 s.

`--timeout 3000` raises CTest's limit per test above 600 s, as a precaution on the shared machine. The slowest shard took 366 s, so the limit wasn't needed.

What the 200 graphs cover:

- 4,175 components, 1 to 40 per graph, with fan-in 10 in 52 graphs;
- 2,615 singletons and 1,560 unique components, 2,932 of them behind an interface;
- 15,661 requests, in all seven forms: `std::unique_ptr` 4,269, `std::shared_ptr` 2,783, `T&` 1,968, `const T&` 1,987, `T*` 1,975, `const T*` 1,992 and `T` 687.

In the Trellis wiring, 153 unique classes have an explicit binding because something requests them as `std::shared_ptr`, 313 are left unbound, and one graph has no binding at all.

## Where the time and the bytes go

### NFR-04: the 100-component graph

The 100-component graph was compiled 3 times per wiring, alternating, with GCC's `-ftime-report` and Clang's `-ftime-trace`. Other agents' builds came and went during these measurements (load 1.7 to 3.2), and the ratios agree with the benchmark's.

| | gcc-13 | clang-18 |
|---|---|---|
| manual | 10.03 s, 383 MB | 11.07 s, 249 MB |
| trellis | 17.32 s (1.73×), 498 MB (1.30×) | 17.81 s (1.61×), 372 MB (1.50×) |
| trellis with `options{.threading = threading::none}` | 15.32 s (1.53×), 458 MB | 15.72 s (1.42×), 367 MB |

This is where Trellis's extra time goes. The profiled compiles are slower than the measured ones, because the profilers cost time.

| Phase | gcc-13 (`-ftime-report`) | clang-18 (`-ftime-trace`, self time) |
|---|---|---|
| Parsing, headers included | 2.59 → 2.82 s (+0.23) | 1.12 → 1.14 s (+0.02) |
| Instantiation and the rest of the front end | 1.93 → 4.66 s (+2.73, "lang. deferred") | 4.02 → 7.43 s (+3.41) |
| Optimization and code generation | 9.35 → 15.90 s (+6.55) | 6.37 → 11.78 s (+5.41) |
| Total | 13.88 → 23.39 s (+9.51) | 11.50 → 20.35 s (+8.85) |

- **Code generation is most of it.** About 69% of the extra time on GCC, and 61% on Clang, goes to optimizing and generating the creation code. For each singleton, Trellis emits:
  - its `resolve` and build functions;
  - the slot's slow path (`construct`, `build_and_publish`, `publish`);
  - the storage's `construct` and `destroy`;
  - a `std::shared_ptr` control block for the `construct_with` allocator.

  The hand-written wiring emits one `std::make_shared` per singleton. The 100-component object has 1,381 functions against 809 by hand on Clang, and Clang's optimizer works on 2,762 functions against 1,618.
- **Instantiation is the other third.** On Clang, the instantiations attributed to Trellis's own templates take 4.1 s:
  - slots and storage, 1.6 s;
  - resolution (plans, nodes, placeholders), 1.2 s;
  - deduced discovery, 0.9 s;
  - the rest, 0.5 s.

  The trace records only events of at least 500 µs, so short standard-library instantiations made inside Trellis's templates count in them, and the standard library's own share drops by 1.4 s.
- **The headers cost almost nothing at this size.**
- **The names are long.** The injector's type lists all 100 bindings, and so does the name of every creation function. The longest symbol is 3,777 characters, and the object holds 519 KB of symbol names (Clang) against 79 KB by hand. Wrapping the bindings in a named module, the idiom of ADR-0010 and `module.hpp` (`struct graph_module : decltype(trellis::make_module(...)) {}`), cuts the names to 200 KB. It saves only 1 to 1.5 s, though: 1.72× (gcc-13) and 1.57× (clang-18), against 1.83× and 1.70× without it, in a run at load 0.75 to 1.89.
- **`threading::none` saves about 2 s** on both compilers, by removing the atomic wait and the retry path. That still leaves 1.53× and 1.42×.

So the ratio is set by the code generated per singleton, not by lookups or the binding map: it holds from 50 components to 100, so nothing grows faster than linearly over that range. Ways to reduce it, none of them in this task's scope, for the owner and the next tasks to choose from:

- move the slot's state machine (`construct`, `build_and_publish`, `publish`) out of the templates on the object type: it needs only the build function, which is already type-erased, and the storage's address;
- share one control-block type between singletons;
- keep the slow path out of line and cold, so the optimizer spends less on it.

### NFR-06: the stripped binary

Binary size doesn't depend on the load.

| | gcc-13 | clang-18 |
|---|---|---|
| manual: file, file-backed sections | 18,736 B, 11,364 B | 14,744 B, 9,621 B |
| trellis | 26,928 B (+8,192), 14,789 B (+3,425) | 27,096 B (+12,352), 13,954 B (+4,333) |
| trellis with `threading::none` | 18,736 B (+0), 13,247 B (+1,883) | 18,840 B (+4,096), 11,451 B (+1,830) |
| trellis with `eager = true` | 26,928 B | 27,096 B |

- **Trellis adds 3.4 KB on GCC and 4.3 KB on Clang** of code, data and unwind tables. The stripped file grows in whole 4 KiB pages. The linker (GNU ld 2.42, which uses `-z separate-code` by default) gives each of these a page-aligned segment of its own:
  - the headers and read-only tables;
  - the code;
  - the read-only data;
  - the writable data.

  The hand-written GCC binary's first segment ends 80 bytes short of a page, so the first added dynamic symbol costs 4 KiB.
- **Most of the excess is the thread-safe slot,** `threading::safe`, the default (plan §16 D6):
  - libstdc++'s `atomic::wait` is inline and futex-based. It brings `syscall`, `sched_yield`, `__errno_location` and `std::__throw_system_error`, plus a 2 KiB waiter table in `.bss`, which takes no file space.
  - The retry after a throwing constructor (ADR-0012 item 6) adds `__cxa_begin_catch`, `__cxa_end_catch` and `__cxa_rethrow`.

  These 7 imports (8 on Clang, which also imports `std::terminate`) and their tables push the first segment over a page. On Clang, the slow path's code also pushes the code segment over another page. With `threading::none` the imports match the hand-written binary's, and on GCC no page is added at all.
- **The rest is per singleton:**
  - the slot's slow path and the storage's functions, about 0.9 KB per singleton type on Clang;
  - the control block's virtual functions, type information and type names, which are longer than `std::make_shared`'s because they name `construct_with<T>`.

### NFR-03: the scenario

On Clang the cost is roughly a third header and two thirds wiring. A traced compile of each program adds 0.36 s, split as follows:

- parsing, 0.10 s;
- the instantiations attributed to Trellis's templates, 0.15 s: discovery, slots and resolution, about equally;
- code generation, 0.15 s;
- standard-library instantiations, −0.06 s, which is noise.

Two of the three alternating measurements put Clang's header at 0.13 to 0.14 s (the third at nothing), and all three put the wiring at 0.17 to 0.32 s. On GCC the header costs 0.06 to 0.08 s and the wiring 0.05 to 0.22 s. The wiring's share comes from the same per-singleton code as NFR-04's, for two singletons.

## Reproducing

```bash
scripts/bench-compile.sh --quick                       # NFR-03 to NFR-06; results in build/bench/compile/
scripts/bench-compile.sh --quick --no-graphs           # the scenario alone (NFR-03, NFR-06)
scripts/bench-runtime.sh                               # NFR-01, NFR-02; results in build/bench/runtime/
TRELLIS_PROPERTY_SEEDS=1-200 ctest --preset clang-18-release -L property   # the oracle on 200 graphs
python3 benchmarks/compile/gen_graph.py --n 100 --variant trellis -o graph.cpp   # one program to profile
```

The breakdowns used scratch scripts built from `benchmarks/compile/run.py`'s functions, with the same flags. The header-alone program is `manual.cpp` with `#include <trellis/trellis.hpp>` added first. The `threading::none` and named-module programs are the generated Trellis program with `make_injector<trellis::options{.threading = trellis::threading::none}>` or with its bindings in `struct graph_module : decltype(trellis::make_module(...)) {}`.
